# Archive requests

A drafting method for requests to archives, civil registries and parishes. These
are research habits, not legal rules or evidence that an office holds a record.
All examples below are invented. This page sends nothing and grants no permission
to disclose personal information or contact an institution.

## Read before editing

Open the current draft and the existing conversation before changing either.
Read the full draft again after editing, not only the changed sentence. Preserve
answered questions and any narrower limits the requester set. Check whether a
reply or a request has already been sent before preparing another one.

## Keep dates and spellings visible

When an examined original act and another source give different dates for the
same event, lead with the date read from the act and retain the other as a sourced
variant in parentheses. Do not silently correct either source. If the act's date
is uncertain, say so; a baptism date is not a birth date, and a same-name act is
not automatically the right person's act. Identify the event and source for each
date before calling them discrepant.

Invented example: "Birth on 14 May 1842 according to the examined act (16 May 1842
in the supplied index)." This is a drafting order, not a rule that every act is
correct or that two records identify the same person.

Include both documented surname spellings in the same request. For example,
"Alfa / Alpha; Delta / Deltta" retains two independent spelling variants without
splitting the work into duplicate requests. Label an unverified spelling as a
search hypothesis, not as an established alias or identity.

## Check that the citation can exist

Compare the cited book's documented year range with the requested event date.
If an index points to Book X but its range excludes the date, preserve the index
clue and flag the mismatch. Do not present the impossible combination as an exact
citation, and do not silently move the event to another volume.

Invented wording: "The index points to Book X, whose stated range is 1830-1840,
while the indexed date is 1842. Possibly in Book X or the next book; please check
the volume and reference." The next book is a possibility, not a confirmed
holding. If its existence or range is unknown, leave that unknown. A folio/page
reference is also not a viewer image number; see [gwt-fetch](fetch.md).

## One request per institution

Use one numbered email for each institution, in its existing thread when there
is one. Re-check the actual recipient and thread before any send. Do not combine
different institutions in To or CC, disclose another office's correspondence, or
start a new thread merely because a draft was revised. A new thread may be needed
if the office explicitly requires it; check that instruction rather than guessing.

Within each institution's request:

1. Put direct-line records first. Within that group, list exact sourced citations
   before open searches. Keep each item numbered and state what is known versus
   what needs a search.
2. Put collateral or other optional records after the direct-line requests, under
   "Additionally, if possible". Within this group, again place exact citations
   before open searches. Do not describe optional work as already agreed.

For a population register or household census sheet, request the complete sheet,
not only a cropped name or row. This is a request, not a claim of access: an office
may restrict or redact information. Seek the relevant page reference and context
without asking for unrelated personal information or assuming permission to share
living people's data.

## Synthetic request outline

> Reply in the verified existing thread for Institution A.
>
> 1. Direct-line birth: [examined act date] ([other date and source] as a variant).
>    Spellings: Alfa / Alpha. Exact reference: [verified book, folio and source].
> 2. Direct-line marriage: an open search within [explicit period and place].
>    Spellings: Delta / Deltta. This is a search hypothesis, not a found act.
> 3. Additionally, if possible: [collateral record with exact citation].
> 4. Additionally, if possible: [bounded open search for another record].
> 5. For the cited household entry, please provide the complete sheet, subject
>    to the office's access and redaction rules.

Replace placeholders only with checked information. Do not send the outline or
invent missing references. A favorable notification is not the certificate:
keep the decision, actual document receipt and any explicitly sourced appeal
instructions separate. Do not invent a deadline, fee, delivery channel or right
of access. Obtain the required approval for recipients and final words before
sending, and approval for any cost before committing to paid work.
