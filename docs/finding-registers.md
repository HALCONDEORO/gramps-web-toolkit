# Finding church and civil registers

Where to look for a baptism, marriage or burial act before 1870 (church) or a civil act (France, Spain), and how to record what you find. This page lists public sources and the order to try them. It copies no data from any of them.

## Spain: parish registers

Before civil registration (1871 in most of Spain) the act is in the parish book. Who keeps a given book varies: some are still in the parish, some are in a diocesan or other church archive, and a diocesan archive may hold copies. Confirm the custody of each book today with the parish and the diocese; do not assume it. Three public sources help you find candidates:

- [Guía de los Archivos de la Iglesia en España](https://www.cultura.gob.es/dam/jcr:f28cc317-7893-40a3-9561-d821b6caf030/archivosiglesia.pdf) (Ministerio de Cultura, PDF, published in 2001): church archives and their holdings. Its addresses and contacts are 2001 data. Check them against the diocese's current site before you write.
- [Censo-Guía de Archivos de España e Iberoamérica](https://censoarchivos.cultura.gob.es/CensoGuia/directorioarchivos.htm) (Ministerio de Cultura): a searchable directory of archives and their holdings. Check the entry's date too.
- [Documentación eclesiástica](https://www.bne.es/es/Micrositios/Guias/Genealogia/busqueda_antepasados/documentacion_eclesiastica/) (Biblioteca Nacional de España): what each kind of church book contains, and a note that diocesan archives may hold copies.

A community finder exists: [archivos-parroquiales by Pablo Granadino Aranda](https://github.com/grana10/archivos-parroquiales). It is under CC BY-NC-ND 4.0 (no derivatives), so this project links to it and cites it and does not copy its code, data or structure.

Order to try:

1. Search the indexed collections (FamilySearch, Genae, PARES). If the parish is not indexed, the images may still exist unindexed.
2. Look for the images. FamilySearch's 2020 [blog post on Explore Historical Images](https://www.familysearch.org/en/blog/explore-historical-images-tool-unlocks-data-in-digital-records) describes browsing images by place and date range. We have not tested the viewer for every collection, and the tool may have changed since. FamilySearch's help page on [finding an image in an unindexed collection](https://www.familysearch.org/en/help/helpcenter/article/how-do-i-find-an-image-in-an-unindexed-collection-in-historical-records) is linked as the official reference; follow its steps there, not a summary here.
3. When the register is on a viewer with no index, use [gwt-books](books.md) to open as few images as possible.
4. If nothing is online, write to the diocesan archive, then the parish. Use an address you have checked as current, one letter per address, and say the act you want (name, approximate date, parish).

## France: civil registers

Civil registers (état civil) are put online by each departmental archive on its own site. [FranceArchives, "État civil numérisé"](https://francearchives.gouv.fr/fr/article/38170) is the official entry point; it does not promise that every department or period is online, so check each archive.

For a wider view there is a dataset on data.gouv.fr, [Principaux corpus d'archives numérisés et mis en ligne par les Archives départementales](https://www.data.gouv.fr/datasets/principaux-corpus-darchives-numerises-et-mis-en-ligne-par-les-archives-departementales). It is not an official or governmental source and is not exhaustive: a contributor published it (Maïwenn Bourdic, updated 15 October 2025). Use it to find leads and confirm on the archive's own site. Record each archive's viewer, captcha and index with [gwt-access](access.md).

## Recording what you find

- Keep the act as a source with a citation (archive, book, page or image, act number). Put what the act says in the tree only when the act names the person.
- Mark an uncertain reading with "(?)" and keep it apart from the certain ones.
- Do not overwrite a date already in the tree because an act reads differently. Add a note that says both and cite both.
- Search for living people nowhere. Search for the dead only when you have confirmed they are dead.

## Pace and terms

Read each site's current terms before automating anything on it; stop on a captcha or a block. [FamilySearch legal page](https://www.familysearch.org/en/legal/terms): consult the current terms there.
