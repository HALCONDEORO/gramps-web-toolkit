# Finding church and civil registers

Where to look for a baptism, marriage or burial act before 1870 (church) or a civil act (France, Spain), and how to record what you find. This page lists public sources and the order to try them. It copies no data from any of them.

## Spain: parish registers

Before civil registration (1871 in most of Spain) the act is in the parish book, and the books belong to the diocese. Three public sources answer "which archive holds this parish":

- [Guía de los Archivos de la Iglesia en España](https://www.cultura.gob.es/dam/jcr:f28cc317-7893-40a3-9561-d821b6caf030/archivosiglesia.pdf) (Ministerio de Cultura, PDF): the diocesan and parish archives, with address and contact.
- [Censo-Guía de Archivos de España e Iberoamérica](https://censoarchivos.cultura.gob.es/CensoGuia/directorioarchivos.htm) (Ministerio de Cultura): a searchable directory of archives, with the holdings of each.
- [Documentación eclesiástica](https://www.bne.es/es/Micrositios/Guias/Genealogia/busqueda_antepasados/documentacion_eclesiastica/) (Biblioteca Nacional de España): what each kind of church book contains and how to read it.

A community finder exists: [archivos-parroquiales by Pablo Granadino Aranda](https://github.com/grana10/archivos-parroquiales). It is under CC BY-NC-ND 4.0 (no derivatives), so this project links to it and cites it and does not copy its code, data or structure.

Order to try:

1. Search the indexed collections (FamilySearch, Genae, PARES). If the parish is not indexed, the images may still exist unindexed.
2. Look for the images: FamilySearch's [Explore Historical Images](https://www.familysearch.org/en/blog/explore-historical-images-tool-unlocks-data-in-digital-records) and its help page on [finding an image in an unindexed collection](https://www.familysearch.org/en/help/helpcenter/article/how-do-i-find-an-image-in-an-unindexed-collection-in-historical-records). Browse by place and date range.
3. When the register is on a viewer with no index, use [gwt-books](books.md) to open as few images as possible.
4. If nothing is online, write to the diocesan archive, then the parish. Use the address in the Guía or the Censo-Guía, one letter per address, and say the act you want (name, approximate date, parish).

## France: civil registers

Civil registers (état civil) are online at the departmental archives, one site each. [FranceArchives, "État civil numérisé"](https://francearchives.gouv.fr/fr/article/38170) links each department's digitised registers. For a bulk view, the data.gouv.fr dataset [Principaux corpus d'archives numérisés et mis en ligne par les Archives départementales](https://www.data.gouv.fr/datasets/principaux-corpus-darchives-numerises-et-mis-en-ligne-par-les-archives-departementales) lists which corpora each archive has put online. Record each archive's viewer, captcha and index with [gwt-access](access.md).

## Recording what you find

- Keep the act as a source with a citation (archive, book, page or image, act number). Put what the act says in the tree only when the act names the person.
- Mark an uncertain reading with "(?)" and keep it apart from the certain ones.
- Do not overwrite a date already in the tree because an act reads differently. Add a note that says both and cite both.
- Search for living people nowhere. Search for the dead only when you have confirmed they are dead.

## Pace and terms

Read each site's terms before automating anything on it. Some, FamilySearch among them, forbid robots. Keep a slow pace, stop on a captcha or a block, and never get around one.
