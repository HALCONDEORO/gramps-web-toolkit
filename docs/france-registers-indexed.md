# France: which registers exist, and what is indexed

This page records, for the French departments this project has worked in, which parish and civil register books are online and which indexes cover them, with the page that says so and the date it was checked. Sites change their coverage, so re-check a line before you rely on it. The page copies no record data from any source and automates nothing. For the national entry points (FranceArchives, the data.gouv dataset) see [Finding church and civil registers](finding-registers.md).

## Indre-et-Loire (37)

Images are online at the Archives d'Indre-et-Loire ([Chercher en ligne](https://archives.touraine.fr/page/chercher-en-ligne)), free to view with no login (checked 8-10 November 2026):

- Parish registers (baptisms, marriages, burials, up to 1792), series 6NUM according to the [FranceArchives finding aid](https://francearchives.gouv.fr/fr/findingaid/f61f1464136028c4f84e8e5e8fbb9e329f0c4148) (published 5 April 2011).
- Civil registers (naissances, mariages, décès, from 1793) and the tables décennales, commune by commune ([NMD index page](https://archives.touraine.fr/ark:/37621/6gwct3fdnp15)).

Indexes over these images (checked 10 November 2026):

- **Geneanet**, [État civil de l'Indre-et-Loire](https://www.geneanet.org/fonds/catalogue/collection/961-Etat-civil-de-l-indre-et-loire/): the collection page says the registers cover 1506 to 1912. The [launch post](https://www.geneanet.org/blog/post/2026/03/letat-civil-de-lindre-et-loire-est-en-ligne) (16 March 2026) says the person-name index runs 1529 to 1951 with 3,136,031 births, 727,007 marriages and 2,185,855 deaths (over 24 million people), and that the images can be linked to a tree.
- **Filae**, [registres paroissiaux d'Indre-et-Loire](http://www.filae.com/ressources/nouveau-registres-paroissiaux-indre-et-loire/): parish registers 1700 to 1792, more than 2 million acts and 2.3 million individuals, one transcription per act with a direct link to the act (page updated 13 July 2026).
- **Centre Généalogique de Touraine**, [Base Touraine](https://www.tourainegenealogie.org/page/3948427-base-touraine): the association's own dépouillements by commune; access is for members.
- **FamilySearch**: the [Indre-et-Loire wiki page](https://edge.fscdn.org/en/wiki/Indre-et-Loire%2C_France_Genealogy) lists civil registration 1542-1900 among the department's record types. Check each collection's own page for its real coverage before relying on it.

Coverage is never complete: a commune or a decade can be missing from the images, from one index, or from both. An empty search means that index holds no match, never that no act exists. For a book with no index, use [gwt-books](books.md); record each archive's viewer, captcha and index behaviour with [gwt-access](access.md).

## Loir-et-Cher (41)

The [état civil page of the Archives départementales de Loir-et-Cher](http://archives.culture41.fr/archives/recherche/etatcivil/) (checked 10 November 2026) lists online: catholic parish registers (baptisms, marriages, burials to 1792), protestant registers (1590-1685 and 1775-1792), registers of religious institutions (to 1791), tables décennales 1793-1952, and civil registers with births and marriages 1793-1925 and deaths 1793 to the 1950s or 1960s depending on the commune. The page says the archive did its own indexing of the acts. Blois is an exception: its commune collection is on the city archive's site, not on the departmental one.

## Nord (59)

The [Archives en ligne page of the Archives départementales du Nord](https://archivesdepartementales.lenord.fr/page/archives-en-ligne) (checked 10 November 2026) lists online: tables décennales, parish and civil registers, population censuses, military matricules, cadastre plans, succession tables and mortgage tables.

## Surname spellings to search with: two worked examples

French indexes match on the spelling that was indexed, so a search plan needs the spellings the clerks actually wrote. The etymologies below come from Geneanet's surname dictionary (entries [LECHAT](https://www.geneanet.org/nom-de-famille/LECHAT) and [BONNET](https://www.geneanet.org/nom-de-famille/BONNET), both checked 10 November 2026); the register sightings are this project's own first readings, without an independent second reading. Spellings are search aids, never identity or merge evidence: feed them to the variant tools ([surname clusters](surname-clusters.md), [variant queries](variant-queries.md)) with their provenance, and keep human review on every match.

**Lechat** (Geneanet rank 1,981; about 118,130 bearers in Geneanet's bases from 1600 to today):

- `Lechat`: the main form, mostly in the West (44, 49, 72).
- `Le Chat`: the split form, noted as Breton. [gwt-rank](rank.md) already treats an exact split as equal ("Le Chat" = "Lechat").
- `Lecat`: the Picard variant, mostly Somme, Oise and Nord-Pas-de-Calais.
- Caution: `Chatelin` is a different surname, not a Lechat spelling. A 1660 baptism names "Gille Chatelin", son of Gille (register [ark:/37621/3q4v2gnt9wcl](https://archives.touraine.fr/ark:/37621/3q4v2gnt9wcl), view 335; first reading). Do not merge Chatelin rows into a Lechat search as identity evidence.

**Bonnet** (Geneanet rank 22; about 1,728,984 bearers):

- `Bonnet`: the common form everywhere; strong in Deux-Sèvres and, at least historically, in Haute-Loire.
- `Bonet`: the Catalan form, mostly Pyrénées-Orientales.
- `Bonnette`: the matronyme (Côte-d'Or in the dictionary), also seen in Touraine: a witness is named "françois Bonnette oncle" at a marriage of 19 May 1725 ([ark:/37621/j9k0qnfdl8cb](https://archives.touraine.fr/ark:/37621/j9k0qnfdl8cb), view 5; first reading).
- Diminutives listed by the dictionary: `Bonneton` and `Bonnetton` (Isère, the Lyon area, Massif Central), `Bonnetain`, `Bonnetin` (71, 69, 42), `Bonnetat` and `Bonnettat` (36, 37, 89, so Indre-et-Loire among them), `Bonnetaud` and `Bonneteaud` (87, 23), `Bonnetot` (03, 42), `Bonnettaz` (73). These are related names, not automatic spellings of one Bonnet line: search them, never merge on them.

## What an index line is worth

An index line is a pointer to an image, not the act itself. Open the image, read the act, and record archive, book, page or image and act number, as [Finding church and civil registers](finding-registers.md) says. A name in an index does not identify a person until the act's dates and parents match.
